# Seven approved lessons → directing obligations

Read the original course files under `Webtoon-AI-Academy/lessons/` whenever a scene requires a nuanced decision. Their approved explanations, examples, and limits take precedence over any abbreviated description here. As of 2026-10-29 days 1–7 have explicit user approval.

## Day 1 — Vertical reading as controlled revelation
The scroll reveals information sequentially, not simultaneously. Identify the reader's question before delaying an answer. Space between panels affects temporal perception, but spacing without a question is often dead time. Compare on actual phone-sized viewport. Use no universal gutter formula.

## Day 2 — Genre, theme, tone, mood and reader promise
Genre indicates expected kind of experience; theme is the underlying question explored through consequences; tone is narrative attitude; mood is a momentary intended feeling. They must not be collapsed into a single adjective. The same factual event can be directed as horror, romance or comedy by different information priorities. Ask what reader is supposed to anticipate and why; avoid stereotypes such as “horror = darkness”.

## Day 3 — Panel's narrative function
Panel functions: establishing place/time; emphasizing evidence; showing change/action; decision/hesitation; reaction/reinterpretation; setup/delay/reveal; relational or atmospheric meaning; transition. A panel may serve several; not every micro-action needs one. Test “what is lost if this panel disappears?” plus emotional and pacing cost. Use closure: audience fills omitted transitions only if causal inference is supportable. Never equate each beat with one panel.

## Day 4 — Prose to beats to visual units
A written sentence does not map 1:1 to a panel. A beat is an operational unit of meaningful change in knowledge, state, choice or relationship. Distinguish events confirmed by narrator, a character's subjective thoughts, reader inference, and newly proposed visual treatment. For internal concepts (“feels betrayed”), select grounded visible signs or text; do not invent contradictory behavior. Track who knows what and when.

## Day 5 — Shots and angles as means, not ends
Shot size determines how much environment/character is visible, from extreme wide to extreme close-up. Camera angle (high/low/eye-level/overhead/tilted), position, viewing orientation and POV are distinct. The image's apparent emotional meaning is context-dependent: high angle is not automatically weakness. Account for geography and basic screen direction; preserve places/props across adjacent panels.

## Day 6 — Composition and visual priority
Composition arranges elements. Focal point is the main attention target. Visual hierarchy prioritizes elements; relationships are context-sensitive. Contrast, scale, placement, isolation, negative space inside the frame, leading lines/gaze, detail, balance, grouping all help. The *position* of a clue is distinct from its *isolation* from competing details. Teach and plan from function → visible evidence → barrier → visual revision, using the corrected lesson text as authority. Do not replace the course's structure with another abstract taxonomy.

## Day 7 — Storyboard as integrated diagnosis
Storyboard is a fast, modifiable visual sequence of shots/panels. A prose shot list is not the storyboard. Sketches may be crude shapes, but must show staging, shot size, composition and reading order. Inspect what the reader knows after every panel. Diagnose whether failure stems from missing fact, wrong panel order, wrong shot, blocked detail or scroll pacing. Finish before costly finished art.

## Working style from the course
The user approved a self-sufficient learning sequence: conceptual anchors, full connected explanation, worked alternative, applied exercise and clear review. For the skill's creative output, write concise but *reasoned* editorial/visual plans rather than verbose lectures. Preserve narrative source faithfully, identify decisions and their alternatives, keep terms clear, and always carry previous scene continuity forward.
